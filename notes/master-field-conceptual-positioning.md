# Where is the large-N gain? The master field vs the bootstrap — a conceptual positioning

**Status:** conceptual/positioning note (markdown). Branch `matrix-master-field`, 2026-07-13.
Companion to `notes/master-field-selection-note.md` (the numerical results). This note answers a
foundational question raised against the "master field does not beat the bootstrap" verdict:
*the master field, as a large-N probability measure, must have some structural feature that a
general-N method lacks — where is that gain?* It is grounded in a four-way verified literature
dig; every load-bearing claim is labelled **[theorem]**, **[established]**, or **[interpretation]**,
and the citation ledger at the end flags what was read primary vs. metadata-only.

**Scope / what "master field" means here.** The *tracial* (single-trace) master field: the state
τ(·) = lim_{N→∞} (1/N)⟨Tr(·)⟩ on the ∗-algebra generated by the matrix variables — a tracial,
type-II₁ / free-group-factor object. This is **distinct** from the *thermal single-trace* operator
algebra (type III₁) that appears in the holographic/Hagedorn setting (Gesteau–Santilli
arXiv:2402.10262; Leutheusser–Liu arXiv:2209.10454); do not conflate them. Conventions: 't Hooft
large-N limit, single-trace normalized moments, factorization ⟨tr A·tr B⟩ = ⟨tr A⟩⟨tr B⟩ + O(1/N²).

## 1. The aim: the program is a reformulation, not (yet) a solution method

The master field was invented to **solve** large-N theories. Gopakumar–Gross state it plainly
(hep-th/9411021, Eq. 1.7): *"the complete solution of large N QCD would be determined if we could
write down four '∞×∞' matrices Āμ."* The mechanism is exactly the measure statement: factorization
makes the variance of every invariant vanish as 1/N², so (p. 2) *"the path integral measure is
localized on a particular set of matrices — the master field — up to a U(N) transformation."*
Yaffe (Rev. Mod. Phys. 54 (1982) 407) makes this a general theorem-scheme: **[established]** N=∞ is
a *classical* system (1/N² plays the role of ℏ), and "solving" reduces to a **classical variational
problem** whose extremum is the master field.

But the two founding papers concede the reduction is representational, not computational, for the
hard cases. Gopakumar–Gross, Conclusions (p. 44), verbatim:

> *"to actually write the master field explicitly would require a knowledge of all the connected
> Green's functions, which is tantamount to solving the theory … all we have done is repackage the
> unknown solution … The only reduction is by a factor of N²."*

— and, crucially, they name the open direction:

> *"this reformulation … suggests new approaches towards solving the theory by constructing the
> master field … explore the operator equations of motion … Do these follow from some kind of
> variational principle that could be the basis for an approximation scheme?"*

Douglas (hep-th/9411025) confirms the program stalled here: explicit master fields exist only for
*"very simple solvable theories such as decoupled multi-matrix integrals or two-dimensional
Yang–Mills,"* and those *"required as input correlation functions computed using other techniques."*

**Verdict.** The master field program is a rigorous **existence/representation** result — N=∞
theories *are* classical (Yaffe) and *are* a single tracial master field on a free operator algebra
(Gopakumar–Gross) — that reduces "solve" to "find/extremize one object." It becomes a genuine
**solution method only for the solvable low-dimensional cases** (QCD₂, Gaussian). For D≥3 it supplies
the language and an existence statement, not an algorithm. **Our loop-residual-minimization +
continuation vehicle is an attempt at precisely the variational-construction direction GG flagged
as open** — not a competitor to the bootstrap.

## 2. The measure's "right feature" is extremality — and it is a theorem

The structural feature that a general-N moment method lacks:

- **Free-probability dictionary [established].** The master field is a tracial state; different
  matrices in it are *freely independent* (Voiculescu, "Limit laws for random matrices and free
  products," Invent. Math. 104 (1991) 201). GG realize it via one creation/annihilation operator per
  matrix on a **Cuntz-algebra Fock space** (âᵢâⱼ† = δᵢⱼ).
- **Concentration [established].** Normalized single traces are deterministic at N=∞ (connected
  correlators ~1/N²; Yaffe RMP 54 (1982) 407; Makeenko–Migdal, Phys. Lett. B88 (1979) 135). The
  measure is a non-commutative "delta function."
- **Extremality = factor state [theorem].** Tracial states form a **Choquet simplex** whose extreme
  points are exactly the **factorial** traces — those whose GNS von Neumann algebra is a *factor*
  (trivial center) (arXiv:2409.09644, Thm 1.1); a type-II₁ factor has a *unique* trace
  (Murray–von Neumann 1937), necessarily extremal. Hence:

  > **large-N factorization ⇔ trivial center ⇔ extreme point of the trace simplex.**

  A convex mixture ½(τ₁+τ₂) of two distinct master fields is a *reducible, non-extremal* trace whose
  GNS algebra has non-trivial center — it carries the O(1) fluctuations factorization forbids. It is
  exactly the kind of unphysical interior point a convex relaxation admits.

**Honest boundary [interpretation].** "extreme ⇔ factor" and "factorization ⇔ trivial center" are
standard operator-algebra facts. The identification *the physically selected master field is the
extreme point, and distinct saddles/branches are the reducible interior points* is the
non-commutative transcription of the algebraic-QFT triad **cluster decomposition ⇔ pure phase ⇔
primary (factor) state**. It is well-motivated and supported in the large-N literature (Makeenko,
hep-th/0001047: distinct master fields for the confined/deconfined phases, one per θ-vacuum), **but
it is a selection *target*, not a theorem that a residual-minimizing optimizer lands on the extreme
point** — see §3 and Maeta's explicit counterexample (§6).

**Where it is rigorously realized.**
- **[theorem, perturbative]** Guionnet–Shlyakhtenko, "Free monotone transport" (arXiv:1204.2182):
  for near-Gaussian multi-matrix potentials the master field exists as an explicit *free transport*
  of the semicircular family, and W*(Z₁,…,Zₙ) ≅ L(𝔽ₙ) — a II₁ factor. The extreme-point picture is
  *literally* realized there.
- **[theorem, 2D]** Lévy, "The master field on the plane" (arXiv:1112.2452): each Wilson loop
  converges in probability to a deterministic limit (simple loop of area t: ⟨W⟩ = e^{−t/2}).
  Dahlqvist–Norris (arXiv:1703.10578): the master field on the **sphere**, which carries the
  Douglas–Kazakov phase transition — a rigorous setting *with* genuine branch structure.
- **No** rigorous existence/uniqueness theorem exists for the strong-coupling higher-D master field —
  precisely the gap the continuation/branch-selection program targets.

## 3. What the bootstrap relaxes, and the structural gain

- **[established]** Kazakov–Zheng (arXiv:2108.04830) obtain convexity by **relaxing factorization**:
  they *"treat the quadratic terms as independent variables and impose the positivity condition …
  relaxing the non-convex conditions involving the non-linear loop equations into convex ones"* — the
  standard SDP lift of the rank-one constraint Q = x xᵀ → [[1, xᵀ],[x, Q]] ⪰ 0. This inflates the
  feasible set (equality → inequality) and admits states that are *not* single factorized master
  fields. It is what makes the program a convex SDP that returns a **bracket**.
- **[established]** Lin (arXiv:2002.08387) and Han–Hartnoll–Kruthoff (arXiv:2004.10212) instead keep
  factorization as an *equality* (strict N=∞) + PSD of the moment matrix; their feasible set is not
  guaranteed convex, and they can converge to a *point* when the saddle is unique — the width then
  comes from the moment cutoff and genuine solution multiplicity, not an explicit relaxation.

**The gain [interpretation, our synthesis].** The physical master field is an *extreme point* of the
convex set the SDP brackets. A convex method can only pin the convex **hull** and certify existence
of *some* representing state (possibly a mixture, via the moment problem); it **cannot single out one
extreme point / one branch.** Continuation from a solvable anchor can. The three ingredients are each
published — Lin's *"converges to a curve … not a point"* for broken Z₂ (2002.08387 §4.1.2); the KZ
convex relaxation; the moment-problem→mixture fact (Zheng thesis, arXiv:2401.00350) — but the
unifying **extreme-point/factor-state** statement is *not* stated verbatim in the surveyed literature;
it is our framing.

**Adversarial caveat [must hold].** This gain **only bites where the saddle is non-unique or has a
flat direction.** For isolated unique saddles the convex program pins them tightly, and island width
at large N is usually dominated by the *moment cutoff*, not by mixtures. "Convexity blurs branches"
is specific to continuous families / spontaneously broken symmetry / moduli — not a blanket failure.

## 4. Where the gain actually lives — three regimes, with the counter-evidence

| Regime | Why the bootstrap is weak or absent | Verified evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Branch / flat-direction selection** | a convex hull cannot isolate an extreme point among a family | Lin 2002.08387 (broken Z₂ → a *curve* of correlators, not a point); Douglas–Kazakov sphere transition (rigorous branch-structured master field, 1703.10578); Maeta 2605.10720 §3.2.1 (residual-min alone is initial-condition-dependent) |
| **Large observables beyond the cutoff** | large loops are *"not related to other WAs by the loop equations … relatively unimportant"* — barely constrained | Kazakov–Zheng lattice bootstrap 2203.11360 (D=3/4 loose; the 4×4 Wilson loop essentially unconstrained at Lₘₐₓ=16). Matches the Vehicle-B area-law finding in the companion note. |
| **Complex action / Minkowski** | positivity — the whole bootstrap engine — is unavailable; sign problem | Maeta 2605.10720 §2.1 (the construction *"has no sign problem"*); Maeta 2601.16099 (positivity replaced by existence of the eigenvalue distribution) |

**Counter-evidence [must hold].** For **low-order observables of a unique Euclidean saddle** the
bootstrap already wins or ties: Lin–Zheng (arXiv:2507.21007) reach **eight significant digits**;
Kazakov–Zheng brackets are ~10⁻⁶–10⁻⁸. There is **no** gain in that regime — which is exactly the
regime the companion note's numerics tested, and why they honestly found none. The gain is *localized*
to the three rows above.

## 5. The two-mode picture: analytic solve vs. numerical construction

- **Analytic mode — the real "solve large-N" [established].** Where structure exists, *constructing
  the master field is a closed-form solve with no finite-N analog*: free probability's R-transform
  (free additive convolution), S-transform (multiplicative), and free cumulants over non-crossing
  partitions (Voiculescu 1986/1987; Speicher, Math. Ann. 298 (1994) 611); the one-Hermitian-matrix
  resolvent / spectral curve (Brézin–Itzykson–Parisi–Zuber, CMP 59 (1978) 35); Gross–Witten–Wadia
  (Phys. Rev. D21 (1980) 446; Wadia, Phys. Lett. 93B (1980) 403); 2D Yang–Mills (Lévy; Douglas–Kazakov
  hep-th/9305047).
- **Perturbative-analytic middle [theorem].** Free transport (Guionnet–Shlyakhtenko 1204.2182) +
  planar-map enumeration (Guionnet–Maurel-Segala, arXiv:math/0503064) solve *weakly-interacting*
  multi-matrix master fields as a convergent series in free semicirculars.
- **Numerical mode — bootstrap-adjacent [our regime].** For generic strong coupling (coupled
  multi-matrix, BFSS-type QM, D≥3 gauge) there is *no* known closed form: the multi-matrix loop
  hierarchy is an **infinite, non-closing, underdetermined** system, and *that underdetermination is
  exactly the bootstrap's PSD gap and the reason numerical construction is needed* — the same
  positive-dimensional variety (dims 3/8/22) the companion note measures. Here the master field is a
  point that competes with, and is bounded by, the bootstrap.

Two clarifications: (i) *solving the loop equations analytically ⇒ having the master field* holds
**only** where the planar hierarchy closes (one-matrix, and models reducible to it) — not generically.
(ii) Topological recursion (Eynard–Orantin, arXiv:math-ph/0702045) takes the N=∞ spectral curve as
*input* and outputs all 1/N corrections, so the master field is the **genus-0 sector**, not the whole
story.

## 6. Positioning of our program

Putting §§1–5 together, our numerical program sits in a clearly-defined place:

1. It attempts **GG's own flagged open problem** — construct the master field by solving its
   operator/loop equations variationally (§1) — not a bracket-narrowing exercise against the bootstrap.
2. Its selection lever (continuation from a solvable anchor) is the **"extra principle" the
   construction demonstrably needs.** Maeta (2605.10720) articulates the construction's advantages —
   (i) a point not a bound (Remark 2), (ii) any observable from one configuration (§2.2.1), (iii)
   existence-as-selection (Remark 4), (iv) reach where positivity fails (§2.1) — *and* the honest
   limit: residual-minimization alone hits spurious configs at F~10⁻³⁰ with initial-condition-dependent
   values (§3.2.1), so branch selection needs a stability/continuation principle. Our continuation is
   one such principle, and it is aimed at the **extreme point** of §2.
3. Related variational constructions: de Mello Koch, Jevicki, Liu, Mathaba & Rodrigues
   (arXiv:2108.08803) and Mathaba, Mulokwe & Rodrigues (arXiv:2306.00935) minimize the Jevicki–Sakita
   *collective Hamiltonian* to build a single master field and read off correlators *and* the
   fluctuation spectrum to high precision — the same (i)+(ii), variationally.

**Honest positioning.** Our contribution lives in the numerical mode: (a) a *quantified* measurement
of the N=∞ under-determination, (b) **continuation-from-a-solvable-limit as the branch selector** —
the extreme-point resolution a convex method structurally cannot perform — and (c) the honest
reach/cost map. It does **not** "solve large-N" analytically (that needs free-probability structure),
and it does **not** beat the bootstrap on the low-order observables where the bootstrap is already
tight. Its defensible edge is exactly the three regimes of §4. The right place to demonstrate the
gain *decisively* is therefore a regime where the bootstrap is provably weak or absent — a
complex-action theory (where positivity, hence the bootstrap, structurally cannot follow), the
Douglas–Kazakov branch structure, or genuinely large loops — **not** the tight low-order Euclidean
observables.
